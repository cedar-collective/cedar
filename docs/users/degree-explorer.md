# Degree Explorer

Open **Explore > Degree Explorer**. Choose one degree, your math starting
course, and your English starting course. A semester board immediately shows
courses and course groups. The latest 2026–2027 catalog examples use one interface:

- Computer Science B.S. and Biology B.S.
- History B.A. (U.S. survey example) and Philosophy B.A. (general major).
- Business Administration B.B.A. (common core and General Management electives).
- Anthropology B.A. (general major with Archaeology and Sociocultural/Linguistic cores).
- Physics B.S. (General Physics concentration).

These are selected routes, not every concentration or alternative in each program.

## Read the semester board

Each column is a semester. Tile color shows how much prerequisite planning a
course needs: green means zero shown prerequisite courses, yellow one or two,
and red three or more, counting earlier courses throughout the chain. Gray
course choices still need their specific prerequisites checked. Category labels
identify major, supporting, English and preparation courses. A paired lecture
and lab share a tile. Light tiles reserve workload for Gen Ed, a minor where
required, or other requirements.

Early **Gen Ed / lower-level elective** allowances now occupy course-sized slots.
Click one to browse typical fall or spring offerings, with course titles, Gen Ed
areas, credits, seats and first-day enrollments. Choose **First semester** to set
a fall or spring start; semester labels and suggestions follow that choice.
Filter the table by area, course or campus. Each row shows the latest matching
season at its campus, college and session, plus its seasonal offering frequency.

Small remainders say **Credits left to allocate**. They are part of the budget,
not complete courses. A choice marked **Needs more credits** requires moving
credits from another slot; the planner still caps semesters at 16 credits.
Suggestions are historical examples from CEDAR's Gen Ed list, not an audit of
what you still need or advice tailored to your major. Courses already represented
by entry routes, requirements and enumerated choices are excluded. Check current
Gen Ed approval, prerequisites and college/major restrictions before choosing.

Related courses line up horizontally: follow English composition, the math
sequence, or a program's course groups across semesters. Gaps keep these rows
aligned, and concurrent courses use separate rows. Sharing a row means the
courses are related; arrows show their actual prerequisite connections.

Named course sets show **Choose 1 of 3 courses**, for example, followed by
**PHIL 333, PHIL 334, PHIL 336**. Longer lists end with an ellipsis when they
exceed the tile's three-line preview. Open the popup for every option's full
title, credits and current catalog link. The Philosophy example shows all five
choice categories; it keeps PHIL 2210 as the individually required course.
Logic's alternatives differ in credits and course level, so that tile says
**Level varies** and its popup explains the three-credit planning allowance.

Computer Science's science cards share a **14-credit minimum**, including two
lectures with their labs. The example reserves four slots: **4 + 4 + 3 + 3**.
The first two cards preview eleven lecture/lab or combined-course options; their
popups list all nineteen individual courses and labs, keeping each pair together.
The other slots list the eleven lecture/combined choices. Choose different courses
for the four slots. Advanced-science alternatives and higher-credit selections
still need review; a lab-free fifth course does not replace required labs.

The general planning pattern puts Gen Ed and lower-level foundations in years
1–2 and upper-level courses in semester 5 or later. Upper-level course groups
follow the same rule. Early allowances say **Gen Ed / lower-level electives**;
later ones say **Upper-level / minor / electives**. These are planning spaces,
not verified Gen Ed or upper-division credit totals. Prerequisites and the
16-credit ceiling still apply, so a sequence can extend the plan.

Every tile shows its prerequisite count and how many later courses it leads
to. Entry courses identify the chain they start. These numbers describe the
selected route after placement, not difficulty or eligibility to register.

A purple diamond marks a different kind of condition. HIST 491 says **2+
upper-level History courses** and **Department permission**, rather than claiming
two particular electives are prerequisites. Selecting it outlines all qualifying
options; any two completed upper-level History courses can meet the coursework
condition. Within a course set, prerequisites are attached to the individual
option: PHIL 358's one-prior-course rule does not apply to every ethics option.

**Suggested Gen Ed** tiles have a lighter surface and dashed border. The specific
course is one option for a general requirement. Named program requirements stay
solid: MATH 1350 is a suggestion for History and a required Phase I course in
Business. The label, as well as the styling, makes the difference visible.

Hover over a course to see its prerequisite/corequisite chain and the courses
that depend on it. Blue lines lead into it; purple lines lead out. Keyboard
focus gives the same preview, and Escape dismisses it. Moving away restores
the last clicked chain. Coursework conditions and unresolved choices keep
their distinct labels instead of acquiring misleading prerequisite arrows.

Click a course (or press Enter/Space) to open enrollment history and first-day
seats first. Touch users can tap directly. Catalog links stay at the top, with
full course and requirement details below the table in the same popup. It
states why the course is present, locates the requirement in the degree
program or a prerequisite section, and quotes a short catalog excerpt. Separate
links open **Degree requirements** and the **Course description**. The source
record identifies the catalog year, retrieval date and review status. Solid
connections mean earlier completion; dashed connections allow earlier or
concurrent study. Some course groups represent choices that still need their
own prerequisites checked. Placement beside another course alone is not a
prerequisite or corequisite claim.

The popup also shows **Offering history & first-day seats** in one standard
CEDAR table: seats offered, filled at first class day, first-day fill percentage,
pressure/coverage, and seasonal frequency. Filter Course or Campus to follow one
component over time. Lecture/lab, teaching campuses, colleges and parts of term
stay separate. **Not offered** means the campus has source data for that term
but no active offering of the course; **Source unavailable** is a missing term.

**Near full** means at least 90% first-day fill. First-day enrollment uses dated
class-list statuses and each section's start date. Missing dates remain
unavailable; census and ever-registered counts are not substituted. Banner retains
only the current status date, so late additions later dropped can overstate the
reconstruction. It is not a frozen first-day roster. Missing source matches,
implausible capacity and counts below 10 are unscored; small counts are suppressed.
The popup's methodology explains the limits. Frequency uses only source-covered
terms in the last three seasonal cycles through settled enrollment. History does
not guarantee future offerings or seats. New course identifiers are not equated
with older numbers. Enumerated alternatives appear as separate course histories;
their seats are not combined. Other course groups need a specific selection.
Press Escape or **Back to roadmap** to close the popup.

For Computer Science, Biology and Physics, starting with Intermediate Algebra
adds MATH 1215 → MATH 1220 → MATH 1250 → MATH 1512.
Starting further along removes the preparation already satisfied by
placement. The example uses the combined trigonometry/precalculus route;
MATH 1230 plus MATH 1240 is another calculus entry route. Biology's example
uses MATH 1512; its MATH 1430 option is not yet interactive.

History, Philosophy and Anthropology show MATH 1350 as an **illustrative Gen Ed
math choice**, rather than a named major requirement. Business explicitly requires
MATH 1350 in Phase I. These statistics routes add only MATH 1215 when starting
with Intermediate Algebra; they do not add calculus. Switching programs preserves
the selected math and English starting points and updates the course route.
BCIS 1110 in Business lists completed math coursework as a prerequisite; test
placement alone is not confirmed to satisfy it. The popup keeps that condition visible.

English can begin with Composition I, the two-semester stretch sequence, or
Composition II. Students who have completed both composition requirements can
remove those tiles and subtract the six completed credits from the remaining
workload. Placement alone does not award prior credit. These selections describe a scenario; they do not determine
placement from a transcript or test score.

Dependent courses move after their prerequisites. Unrelated coursework remains
available. The planner aims for the usual **15-credit load** and never proposes
more than **16 credits**, including other degree work. This example adds
preparation to its degree-credit planning target; reviewed overlap or transfer
credit may reduce the estimate. Other degree allowances fill toward 15 first,
then use 16 when needed. The plan adds semesters when the workload or sequence
requires them. These allowances are not a completed degree audit.
On smaller screens, scroll within the board to see later semesters.

This is an illustrative plan, not a graduation guarantee. Semester positions are
authored planning suggestions; prerequisites come from selected catalog routes.
Admission restrictions remain in course details, and eligibility is not computed.
Specific electives, science alternatives, course offerings, transfer credit,
waivers and all university/college requirements need review. Biology's catalog
totals and breadth wording still need reconciliation. Current CSCI identifiers
are not automatically equated with historical CS courses.

Business's common-core and elective totals conflict within its page, and its
concentration flag conflicts with the stated General Management option. Anthropology
also explicitly permits a general major despite its concentration flag. Its selected
course combination totals 37 major credits against the 36-credit minimum. Physics's
structured General Physics concentration has 21 credits, while introductory text
says 18. These conflicts and selections remain in the workload/source notes.

## Observed course paths

Open **Pathways > Course paths**. Define the student population with the shared
Pathways controls, choose delivery campuses and enrolled-term count, and click
**Show course paths**. This is a separate descriptive view of what students
actually take. Changing the population clears its old result.

Term one is first observed undergraduate enrollment, anchored before campus
filtering. Summer is included; gaps do not advance this enrolled-term clock.
The report stops at settled enrollment and respects the population's leaving
window. Earlier coursework can appear before major declaration. The scope note
reports left-boundary history, unmapped coursework and suppressed cells.

Each cell shows the share of students participating at that campus and enrolled
term who take a kind of coursework. Registered students and late drops count;
early drops and waitlists do not. Multiple courses in one group count the student
once. Groups can occur together, so shares need not sum to 100%. Positive cells
require 10 students and campus/term denominators require 20. Course details use
the same guards. Student identifiers do not enter the browser payload.

These groups describe coursework, not Gen Ed eligibility or completion. This
view does not infer individual transition paths or impose today's degree rules
on historical students. Neither prototype changes enrollment forecasts.
