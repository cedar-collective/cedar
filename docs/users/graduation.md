# Graduation
{: .fs-9 }

**Where do students leave without a degree, and how far along were they?**
{: .fs-6 .fw-300 }

---

Graduation is the institution-wide view of the students who stopped enrolling
and hold no degree. It lives under **Bird's Eye > Graduation** and builds when
you open it. Choose a student level, the students' home campuses, and how many
credits count as far along: 90, 115 or 120.

## Who is counted

- **Last term.** The last term a student was enrolled at the university, at
  any level. The level and home campus in scope are that term's.
- **Left.** The two regular terms after the last term passed with no
  enrollment (summer is not a step), and the student holds no degree of any
  kind. A last term too recent to see two terms past is not judged; everyone
  still enrolled is in that group, and the page says how many there are.
- **Not known.** A student who finished at another institution counts here as
  having left.

## How Far Along They Were

One bar for each credit band, from under 30 to 120 or more. Credits are the
total on the registrar's record when the student stopped, with transfer and
test credit. Where that record was taken before the next term began, the
credits completed in the last term are added; the section's method says for
how many students.

## Term by Term

The far-along students by the term they stopped after. More students stop
after a spring than a fall, so compare a term with the same term a year
earlier.

## Where They Stood

For each credit band: the share ever Pell-eligible, and the share who no
longer met two general standards for aid, a cumulative GPA under 2.0 and 160
attempted credits or more. CEDAR holds no record of anyone's aid. These columns
say who would no longer have met the standard, not who lost aid.

## By College and Major

The far-along students by their primary major in their last term, and that
major's college. A major with fewer than 10 such students is counted with all
other majors.

## Not yet on the page

What each student's major still required. That needs a reviewed requirement
file for each program; see the roadmap.
