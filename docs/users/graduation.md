# Graduation
{: .fs-9 }

**Where do students leave without a degree, and how far along were they?**
{: .fs-6 .fw-300 }

---

Graduation is the institution-wide view of the students who stopped enrolling
and hold no degree. It lives under **Bird's Eye > Graduation** and builds when
you open it. Choose a student level, the students' home campuses, and how many
credits count as far along: 90, 115 or 120.

## Who is counted

- **Last term.** The last term a student was enrolled at the university, at
  any level. The level and home campus in scope are that term's.
- **Left.** The two regular terms after the last term passed with no
  enrollment (summer is not a step), and the student holds no degree of any
  kind. A last term too recent to see two terms past is not judged; everyone
  still enrolled is in that group, and the page says how many there are.
- **Not known.** A student who finished at another institution counts here as
  having left.

## How Far Along They Were

One bar for each credit band, from under 30 to 120 or more. Credits are the
total on the registrar's record when the student stopped, with transfer and
test credit. Where that record was taken before the next term began, the
credits completed in the last term are added; the section's method says for
how many students.

## Term by Term

The far-along students by the term they stopped after. More students stop
after a spring than a fall, so compare a term with the same term a year
earlier.

## Where They Stood

For each credit band: the share ever Pell-eligible, and the share who no
longer met two general standards for aid, a cumulative GPA under 2.0 and 160
attempted credits or more. CEDAR holds no record of anyone's aid. These columns
say who would no longer have met the standard, not who lost aid.

## By College and Major

The far-along students by their primary major in their last term, and that
major's college. A major with fewer than 10 such students is counted with all
other majors.

## The Courses Their Program Requires

For the far-along students in a program with a degree plan on file, choose a
plan to see each course it names and how many of those students:

- **tried it here and never passed**: a grade or a late withdrawal, and no
  passing grade. This is the count to read. It says a student met the course
  and did not get through it. Everyone counted left without a degree, so it
  is how many of the leavers had failed the course, not how many of the
  course's students went on to leave, and not a claim that the course is why;
- **passed it here**;
- **have no attempt here**. This is not the same as still required. Most
  students who leave far along earned most of their credits somewhere else, and
  the class lists begin in 2019, so the course may have been met by transfer or
  test credit, or in an earlier term. The section says how many credits these
  students earned here.

The plan is the current catalog's, not the one each student entered under.
Choices, elective space, general education and the math and English starting
courses are not read. A course no one had taken under the plan's number by
then shows a dash. A plan with fewer than 10 such students is not listed.

## At 160 Attempted Credits

The students whose attempted credits passed 160 with no degree yet. 160
attempted credits is a general standard for aid; CEDAR holds no record of
anyone's aid, so this is who would no longer have met the standard.

- **Where the credits came from.** One bar for the average student at that
  point: credits brought from elsewhere, credits completed here, and credits
  attempted here and not completed (failed, withdrawn, incomplete or repeated).
- **What happened next.** For the term they passed it and each regular term
  after: the share with a degree, still enrolled, and not enrolled. Each bar
  counts only the students who can be followed that far.

A student whose record began before the class lists, or who was already past
160 in their first term on record, cannot be placed and is counted in the
section's method. For the same reason the number passing each term is not
shown as a trend.

## Still Here, and Moving Forward?

Retention says a student came back. This section asks whether they are also
earning credits. Each bar is one fall's first-time students, read as a year
begins (the second, third, fourth or fifth), and split into:

- **awarded a degree** by then;
- **enrolled, on pace for four years**: 15 credits completed here for each
  regular term since entering, summer credits included;
- **enrolled, on pace for six years**: 10 credits a term;
- **enrolled, slower than that**;
- **enrolled, earlier credits not on record**: students whose first term here
  is the first term of the class lists, which is the whole of the first class;
- **not enrolled**.

If the enrolled share grows from class to class while the on-pace share does
not, students are being kept and not moved forward. Credit brought from
elsewhere is not counted, so a student who arrived with credit is further
along than shown. Transfer students are not in these classes.

**Credits earned, term by term** draws a line for each class: the credits the
middle enrolled student had earned here before each term, against the
four-year and six-year paces. Students who left are not in a line, so it can
rise because slower students left.
